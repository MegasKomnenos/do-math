### What kind of statement each sentence is

The document mixes statements of different kinds, and a reader who is about to rely on one of them needs to know which kind it is.

A **user decision** is something the user said, or something put to the user that the user answered or approved. Where the user's own words exist they are given, in the language the user used, with an English gloss where the words are Korean, and with their source: the session and the time of the message, or the memory note that records it. Times of messages are UTC. Times of commits and of events on the machine are KST, nine hours ahead, and are marked so. An answer of one word to a particular question or item is a decision on that item. An approval of a long proposal as a whole is a decision too: the agreement of twenty-five decisions for the fourth generation, the forty criteria of 7 November and the agreement for the finalize pipeline were worded by an assistant and approved by the user, and they bind. Where an approved text and the user's own words differ on one matter the user's words govern, and a later decision governs an earlier one. A user decision binds the run.

An **assistant's choice** is something an AI session decided that the user was not asked about, or was told of and did not answer. Much of the detail of the skill is of this kind: the particular way in which a decision of the user was carried out in code or in wording. Such a choice does not bind the run. It may be revisited where there is a reason, within everything the user did decide. In section 4 a statement is a user decision when it quotes the user, gives the time of the user's message or the memory note that records it, cites one of the forty criteria by its letter and number, or names an agreement as its source; where the text says that a session chose or added something, that is an assistant's choice. Where an approved passage of a guide is concerned, what binds is what the passage says and not its wording: it can be reworded, moved or tidied like any other passage so long as nothing it says is lost. Sentences of sections 3 and 4 that draw a consequence for corrections from the user's words ("so", "therefore", "a correction should not") are this document's reading of those words. The consequences that bind are the ones the freeze document states as invariants. Section 4.12 lists the choices most easily mistaken for decisions, because past sessions more than once took their own earlier wording for the user's intent.

An **observed fact** is something an investigator checked in a file, in the Git history or on disk on 25 November 2026. ## 2. What do-math is

This section is a summary for orientation, drawn from `SKILL.md` and the guides as they stood on 25 November. It is not a standard against which a guide is judged.

do-math is a skill by which an AI agent, at a user's request, sets up and runs autonomous mathematical research on one proposition. The agent who talks with the user is called the **operator**. It fixes the proposition with the user, prepares a research **instance** (a directory with its own Git repository), and starts a detached program, the **runner**, which carries the research forward after the conversation ends. The proposition the instance exists to settle is its **root**.

The knowledge of an instance is its **canon**: one Lean 4 library, with a written **record** beside each declaration. A declaration together with its record is an **element**. A definition is an element; so is a statement, whether or not it yet has a proof. A proof is either complete in Lean, or it is written in words in the record while its Lean proof names the elements it rests on and ends in `sorry`. Review judges each proof twice, whether it is correct and whether a correct proof is accepted, and a statement accordingly has one of four **standings**: no proof; proof not confirmed; correct but held back; accepted. A statement is **settled** when one of its proofs is accepted and every statement that proof names is settled. An element is **formal** when its proof is complete in Lean and depends, through everything it uses, only on the allowed axioms (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`; never `sorryAx`). The **frontier** is the view computed from the root downward along what each proof names, showing every statement that is not settled. The instance is complete when the root, or its negation, is settled by a formal proof.

The runner launches fresh AI conversations called **sessions**, each in one of six roles. An **attempt** reads the frontier and chooses work that remains. An **explore** session holds a **question**, something the research wants to understand, and develops understanding of it; question `q0` is the root problem as a whole. A **formalizer** writes the Lean proof that a settled statement's written proof still lacks. A **peer** takes over the work of one of those three, continues it, and writes the **candidate**, the proposed change to the canon. A **review** session has three independent sub-reviewers examine the candidate, compiles it itself, and judges each element. An **organizer** joins what is the same by proving agreement in Lean, rearranges modules and merges questions. To avoid two sessions doing the same work, a session takes the **lease** of a statement and registers the **section** of it that it will work on, which is the whole statement or an exact part of what it asserts; an explorer takes a question in the same way. A registered section holds until the work on it has been published. Review judges and does not gate: useful imperfect work is published with review's judgments.

Each session runs under a **session host**, a process that stays alive across the session's turns and resumes the same conversation when a turn ends before the work is done. All Lean compilation in an instance goes through that instance's **Lean broker**, a service that schedules compiles within memory and thread budgets and keeps a cache of compiled modules. Three further commands work from a canon and are run by the operator, outside the runner. `depmap.py` draws the **dependency map**. `paper.py` (the **paper command**) has sessions write a mathematical paper from the canon and typesets it; its sessions are not research sessions, and a run of it is called here a paper run. `finalize.py` (the **finalize pipeline**, installed on 25 November) turns a finished research, one whose root has a formal proof, into a **package**: a folder `solved/<name>/` that holds the Lean library reduced to what the chosen proof needs, its records, the paper, the dependency map, a history of the research, an archive of its knowledge, a README and a licence, and that can be published as it is. The pipeline runs its own sessions in a fixed order of stages, each stage closed by a check the program makes (its exit check), and it calls the paper command for the paper. `package.py` holds the part of the pipeline's work that needs no session.

Some further terms recur in this document. A session is one conversation of an AI command-line client, either Claude Code (`claude`) or the Codex CLI (`codex`); the guides and notes call that client the CLI and a session's CLI process a worker. What a session is given to read at its start is its **pack**. The check that the runner makes of a finished session's output, of its form and its references and not of its mathematics, is the **transport check**. The **index** is the table the program extracts from the compiled library after each publication: for every declaration its kind, its statement, what it uses and the axioms it depends on. Besides its record, a statement carries its **approaches tried**, one entry for each line of attack pursued on it with where that line stopped, and a question carries its **understanding so far**. A record gives its element a **weight**, unit or step, by which the dependency map and the paper decide what to show as a result in its own right. A **concern** is a challenge that a session reports against an element's proof; a **finding** is a judgment of review that withdraws a proof's confirmation (the paper command and the finalize pipeline use the same word for something else, a single thing that a session reading a manuscript or a package reports; the two have nothing to do with each other); a **comprehensive review** is an assignment of the review role to examine questioned proofs and their uses. A statement whose negation is settled is **refuted**, and a statement and its negation that are both settled are **contested**. A **standing input** is a text that the operator submits with the `input` command and that every session launched afterwards receives. It is how the user's conditions reach a running instance, and the notes also call it a standing condition. The roles that produce work for a peer to take over (attempt, explore, formalizer) are the producing roles; when a candidate cannot be published, the runner assigns a new peer to the same work, a replacement peer. The **literature server** is scholarly-mcp, a server through which sessions search for and fetch mathematical literature; settings and status output call it `scholarly`.

Two research instances are named throughout. The **parking** instance (주차함수: that a certain count attached to parking functions is unimodal) belonged to the third generation, which also required a formal Lean proof of the root, kept in Lean files beside its prose records. It ran from 19 to 23 November, proved its root formally, and was the instance through which the user gave most of the conditions that the guides now carry. Its proof was afterwards put into the form of a fourth-generation canon with `init`. An instance made in that way has a canon and no sessions; this document calls it a canon-only instance. The paper and map commands have been run on canon-only instances of the parking proof and of a second proof of the same result, which the notes call the staircase proof. The **sunflower** instance (해바라기: the Erdős–Rado sunflower conjecture) is the first instance of the fourth generation to import Mathlib. It has run since 22 November and is running now (section 6).

The skill consists of an entry point for the operator (`SKILL.md`), reference documents (`reference/`), one guide per role (`roles/`), short texts shown to a session when it is resumed (`resume/`), the instructions given to the sessions of the finalize pipeline (`finalize/`), the program (`runtime/`) with its tests (`runtime/tests/`), and a collection of real mathematical papers used as models by the paper command (`exemplars/`). Section 7 maps these in detail.

The skill has been rebuilt several times, and the notes speak of **generations**. In the third generation, which ran until 21 November, knowledge was kept as prose records, "nodes" for exact statements, "relations" between them and a glossary. The **fourth generation**, committed on 22 November as `ceb38646`, replaced all of that with the Lean library described above. The fourth generation is what this run covers. The directory `.agents/skills/do-math/` is a copy of the skill, generated by a script from `.claude/skills/do-math/` for another AI runtime; the notes call it the **mirror** and call the original the **canonical tree**.

### What "doc bloat" means here

On 21 November, after a session had added several passages to the guides, the user asked for a tidying and said what kind (session `1f8bb240`, 16:00:21Z):

> "전반적으로 내용에서 지금의 스타일과 전달하는 내용 및 디테일들은 유지하면서도 중복이라던지 과하게 장황한 것 등은 정리해보고 싶어. 그러니까, 내용을 압축하는 것이 아니라 내용을 유지하면서 그에 더해 불필요한 군더더기를 덜어내는"

(Keep the present style, the content conveyed and the details, and tidy away duplication and excessive verbosity; that is, do not compress the content, but keep the content and remove the unnecessary padding on top of it.) The session then removed three kinds of text: an explanation restated in one document when its main statement stood in another document that the same reader receives, repetition within one document, and connecting sentences that carried no content (memory note `M-0734`, correction 5). It applied this only to the passages it had itself written; the rest of the guides were never tidied in this way.

On 22 November, starting a revision of the guides, the user set the aim in one sentence (session `20ae7c1d`, 05:36:20Z): "Let's focus on improving the guidance in both effectiveness, efficiency, while retaining delicate and accurate details".

On 24 November an audit of the whole skill for over-engineering (below, the over-engineering audit) reported to the user that the guides each session receives are large (a starting pack of 430 to 730 KB once the state of the research is included) and that "Twelve rules are stated in two to five documents each". The user answered that item with one word, "intended" (session `20538f79`, 13:46:18Z). The memory note of that day records the item among the things the user kept: the composition of the guides that sessions receive (`M-0772`).

These statements pull in two directions, and the run has to hold both. On 21 November the user wanted duplication and verbosity tidied away, with the content kept. On 24 November, told that the same rules stand in several documents and that the packs are large, the user called the guides as each session receives them intended. On 25 November the user asked this run to reduce bloat in the documents.

The reading this run adopts is the following, and the freeze document makes it the rule of the run. It is the driving session's reading of the three statements together, and not a further statement of the user's. The guides are meant to be rich and detailed, and they are not to be cut down for their size. Which guides each role receives is the intended design. So is the statement of one rule in more than one guide: that is exactly what the audit put to the user, and the user called it intended. A rule stated in two guides is therefore not bloat, and is left. What counts as bloat is text that gives its reader nothing: an explanation, an account of a case or an argument that the same reader has just been given in the same pack and that is given again with nothing added; repetition within one document; sentences that only connect; and, after this week, text about something that no longer exists. Removing bloat never means leaving out a condition, an exception, a step of an argument, a criterion of judgment or a case, and a correction that removes or merges text has to show that the reader of the removed text still receives what it gave. `runtime/context.py` decides what each reader receives (section 7.2). The audit's notes (section 12) say which rules it counted.

Within one document a passage said twice is bloat whatever its kind, and what is kept is the place where the reader needs it. Repetition across guides is different, and it is judged by what is repeated. For the sessions of the paper command and of the finalize pipeline, which have no pack, the same judgment is made on what the program hands to one session (section 7.2). Words that a session follows or judges by in that place (what to do, what not to do, what to look at in judging) are left wherever they stand, however often they are stated. An explanation of where something is or how it works, an account of how something came about, or an argument for a rule, carried with the same content in two guides of one pack, is bloat where the later place adds nothing for that role; one place is kept and the other is removed or shortened to a pointer. The facts that bear on it are who reads each of the two places, whether they are read together in one pack or at different moments (a resume text is read hours after the guides), and whether the second place adds anything. Where it is not clear which kind a passage is, the passage is left as it is. One case needs no weighing: two statements of one matter that no longer say the same thing are a defect, whatever view is taken of repetition.

### What the user asks of tests

The user's requirements for the tests of this skill were given for an earlier verification run and recorded in English in its freeze document (`임시/do-math_검증_20261105/동결.md:56`):

> "(d) tests of the actual intended runtime behavior rather than a divergent imitation, assertions capable of failing when behavior deviates, and simple, cost-effective tests without unnecessary machinery; (e) complete coverage within each test module and across the suite, without needless overlap, redundancy or combined complexity."

The three test-related goals of the present request are these same requirements seen from the side of their failure: tests that overlap or carry machinery they do not need ("test bloat"), behaviour that no test would notice breaking ("test coverage"), and tests that cost more time than what they check requires ("test efficiency"). Sections 7.4 and 8 give the recorded cost of the suite, about thirty-six minutes for one sequential run of its twenty-three modules at the commit from which the run started, and where the time goes; the user asked on 22 November "why do the tests take so long" (session `3750b5d4`, 19:20:27Z) and nothing was changed in response. Section 11 gives the repository's standing criteria for judging a test.

These goals can pull against one another, and one way of trading them is closed: merging two tests into one so that an assertion which only one of them made is lost. The judges of an earlier run rejected such a proposal, and the freeze document of this run makes it a rule. Reducing a test's cost has to preserve the starting state and the real path it exercises, and not only its assertions.

### What the user asks about running tests and other checks

During this week the user stated a principle about checks, after watching sessions repeat them (session `20538f79`, 24 November 12:13:09Z):

> "a lot of the procedures are inefficient and repetitive. for example, subagent running the tests before reporting, then the receiving agent running the same ... tests all over again, running expensive builds only to run builds again like seconds later, running entire-scope verification while changing pieces of it which incur running entire-scope verification again afterwards, running whole tests after changing some doc that PHYSICALLY CANNOT CHANGE TEST RESULTS, a lot of the procedures done ad-hoc are more like a facade of work"

The session's own summary of the principle, which the user let stand, was: "A check is worth running only when its result is not already settled. That means something it depends on has changed since it was last run on the same bytes." The limit on test runs in the present request follows from this, and from the fact that the machine is also carrying live research (section 6). In this run the repairers, the mergers and the judges do not run the tests. The driving session runs tests at most once in each window of four rounds (rounds 1 to 4, 5 to 8, 9 to 12, 13 to 16), after the last round of the window or of the run, and then only the modules whose result is not settled: the modules that can be affected by a file that has changed since they last ran. A change to a guide alone can count, because some tests assert sentences of the guides. Between those runs a correction to the program or to a test is therefore accepted on reading alone, which is why a correction that changes behaviour has to carry the change to the test that asserts that behaviour, and why a judge who cannot follow a change to its end by reading rejects it.

### 4.1 What the fourth generation is for

The user's brief for the rebuild (session `d778f997`, 21 November 15:14:03Z; the same text in session `e13e3c64`) asked for five things: that the problem and every proposition and relation around it be expressed in Lean and laid out, not only a few key statements; that the record of mathematical content, one per Lean element and native to Lean's structure, be separated from the record of process; that sessions be able to traverse the canon mechanically and deterministically, and be told to, so that repeated work drops; that a script calling AI sessions produce a paper and its PDF from the canon; and that the use of Lean be fused into the whole of the work, "다만 자원 소비가 효율적인 방식으로" (but in a way that consumes resources efficiently). The brief ends with a principle that governs how old and new are to be weighed:

> "만약 지금 기존의 절차와 이 새로운 제안이 서로 충돌한다 느낀다면 애시당초 기존의 절차를 개선하는 것이 목적이기 때문이야. 그러니 어떻게 하면 기존의 절차를 이 새로운 제안에도 불구하고 유지할 수 있을까, 는 애시당초 방향이 틀린 고민인거지."

(If the existing procedure and this proposal seem to conflict, that is because improving the existing procedure is the purpose; asking how to keep the old procedure in spite of the proposal is the wrong question from the start.)

Two requirements from the design conversation bear directly on bloat inside an instance. The user wanted records not to keep accumulating, "런타임이 길어져도 bloat이 최소화되도록" (so that bloat stays minimal even as the run grows long; 16:30:52Z), and wanted what sessions learn to accumulate by the object it concerns and not one record per session (16:34:29Z). And on unformalized results the user set a direction (17:37:23Z): the rule is not "discard what is not expressed in Lean" but "express it in Lean and keep it", and expressing things in Lean is to be a central step of the work from its start, "자연어 처리의 위에 더해지는 chore가 아니라" (not a chore added on top of work done in natural language).

### 4.2 The sessions judge; the machine does not

The oldest and most often repeated of the user's requirements is that judgments of mathematical meaning belong to the sessions and that the program records and carries out what they decide (criteria B1 to B4). The program refuses only what a machine can decide: that Lean confirms a statement is the same as another or its negation, that something compiles, which axioms a proof depends on, names and hashes, the form of files, who holds a lease. A correction that makes the program judge mathematical content, the faithfulness of a statement, the sufficiency of an argument or the meaning of an overlap between sections goes against this.

There is no planning role and no assignment of work: exactly six roles, and attempts choose their own work (criterion A2).

The limits placed on sessions rest on their cooperation, and this is deliberate. When a comparison of the skill against outside standards reported that sessions can write to every file the checks rely on, the user answered (session `20ae7c1d`, 22 November 05:36:20Z): "restrictions are intentionally reliant on session's cooperation for effectiveness, efficiency, and simplicity." The absence of an enforcing mechanism is therefore not a defect, and a correction should not add enforcing wording or machinery.

The same holds for the operator. During a verification run on 10 November the user gave this instruction, which later freeze documents have carried forward (original in `임시/do-math_검증_20261110-2/동결.md:76-80`):

> "there is no need to consider a case of intentionally destructive and hostile operator or issues that would arise only in such a circumstance. on the contrary, if such considerations lead to rule-ification of what should be sessions' semantic judgement, restrict valuable degree of freedom, or overengineer the system into unnecessary complexity, or create rules that does not improve the effectiveness of research and/or decrease its effectiveness, such considerations are to be actively addressed"

So a problem that arises only when the operator deliberately breaks the system is not a defect, and code, text or tests that exist only to guard against such an operator, and that cost freedom or simplicity, are themselves defects.

On orchestration the user said (session `3171182f`, 23 November 00:34:54Z) that an orchestrating agent either knows the details of every area it delegates, in which case it need not delegate, or does not, in which case it gives directives that constrain the worker who meets the actual detail; "So, it is better to just set governance rules and tools that help agents orchestrate themselves, in my opinion." A correction that adds central direction of the sessions' work runs against this.

When sessions evaluated do-math this week the user corrected them more than once for judging by proxies and for treating deliberate positions as failures: evaluate "without mistaking any of its key qualities as failures or evaluating trade-offs as mistakes" (session `5172428b`, 22 November 14:18:33Z), and "Don't rely on proxies as sole basis of your evaluation; actually read what is there, what is implemented, and judge based on what actually is" (15:35:11Z). This document takes the same standard to apply to what a repairer calls a defect.

### 4.3 How the guides are written

**Knowledge for judgment, not rules.** The guides that worker sessions read (`roles/`, `resume/`, and the documents of `reference/` in their packs) are meant to give a session the understanding from which it judges, not conditions and commands (criteria C1 to C3, F1 to F5, G2). The operator's documents (`SKILL.md`, `reference/operations.md`) describe procedures and commands and are not under this limit. Nor are the method document of the paper command and the instruction files of the finalize pipeline: the first is the fixed way that the user asked a script to supply (section 4.10), and the second are the instructions of a program that holds the checks itself (section 4.11). A correction does not add if-then rules, lists of things to refuse, closed lists of considerations, mandatory fields for justification, or gates. A choice left to the session is written as reasons, costs and open examples. This limit does not apply to the contracts that a machine or another role consumes: the form of an output file, the arguments of a command.

On 21 November the user decided that the way of working and review's criteria for accepting a proof should nonetheless be written down explicitly, and in what manner (session `1f8bb240`, 14:37:22Z):

> "명시적 규범으로 적자, 다만 판단의 여지 없이 hard하게 적용하는 것이 아니라 soft하게 그를 guide하는 방식으로"

(Write them as explicit norms, but not applied hard with no room for judgment; in a way that softly guides it.) They are to be phrased directly: "the user asks ... 가 아니라 you should ... 식으로" (not "the user asks ..." but "you should ..."; 15:49:50Z). The essay `reference/what-research-is-for.md` was to be left as written, and its relation to the new norms explained in a paragraph of `reference/common.md` (14:44:01Z). That essay and `reference/user-writing-preferences.md` carry the user's own account of what research is for and of how the user wants things written. Earlier verification runs treated both as the user's texts and left them unchanged, and they are not among the files this run covers.

**English, with no reason given.** Everything inside an instance is written in English: "그냥 다 영어로 하자 인스턴스 내부 모든걸" (14:17:33Z). The user gave no reason and the guides state none; a correction should not supply one. The user's words reach an instance only as an English translation, and the operator keeps the original outside it (the same session, 14:30:50Z).

**What the user's conditions say, and nothing added.** In the parking instance, several things that review sessions and other sessions did wrong came from sentences the operator had added around the user's words: a reason the user had not given, a broader reading than the user meant. ### 4.4 What counts as proved

Three things are fixed by the agreement and by criterion A3. The default allowed axioms are `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, and `sorryAx` is never allowed. A complete Lean proof brought by a session is confirmed only after review has compiled the candidate itself. And the standard for the instance's completion, a formal proof of the root or of its negation, is not to be lowered.

This week the question of what must be trusted beyond Lean was decided. A second check of formal proofs outside the program's own index, here called the formal judge, had been added on 22 November with the user's approval: the compiled declarations were exported and compared by an external program, `lake comparator`. On 24 November, after it had stopped a live runner, the user asked what it was for and then decided (session `20538f79`, 12:42:15Z and 12:46:54Z):

> "is that really necessary? if statement is true and if lean is compiled then it is proved, as long as we do it right. It feels wasteful to create this elaborate setup to prepare for a case where it is not done right, instead of just doing it right"
>
> "I feel we are very much overcomplicating a very much simple issue, on the issue of lean's reliability"

The formal judge was removed, and the program now takes the axioms a declaration depends on from Lean's own report. A correction should not reintroduce a formal judge in any form: an export of the compiled declarations, a second program that checks them again, or a comparison by an external comparator. The program still compares the root's statement and the definitions under it with the instance's first commit when the root becomes formal (the "root check"). That check sees less than the formal judge did: it does not see a private helper of a definition, nor a definition whose unchanged text Lean now reads as a different term. The user was told and has not ruled on it; it is a known limit, not a defect to repair by rebuilding what was removed.

### 4.7 How a defect is to be repaired

The user has said a good deal this week about what a good repair is, mostly in response to repairs that were not.

**Find the cause and repair that.** Dispatching an agent on an incident, the user asked for "the real, root cause and full solution, not something shallow and surface, something that is simple, effective, and robust, and accurate" (session `0860eb60`, 24 November 11:07:50Z).

**Do not build a safeguard for a state the system should not reach; prevent the state.** The agent's repair for that incident included a path that recovered when a folder the runner needed had been deleted. The user refused it (11:49:12Z):

> "so the issue is that it may be removed, right? and it is a case that should not happen in the first place. I think building a failsafe for that exception is the wrong path to take. the better path is to just avoid it, plain and simple, I think"

The only thing that could delete the folder was the system's own clean-up rule, so the rule was changed and the recovery path, its test and its sentence in the guide were taken out. The note of that session states the lesson as a question to ask of any failure state: who creates this state, and if the system does, repair the rule first.

**Simplicity.** "Simplicity is best." and "If a solution for any problem can be implemented before any runs, at this time now, then just be repeated reliably without much thoughts, that is the best." (session `20538f79`, 24 November 14:32:28Z). That is: a solution that can be put in place once, ahead of time, and then simply relied on is better than one that needs judgment each time it is used. The formal judge was removed on the same ground.

**Delete what is not used.** "delete what is not used" (session `20538f79`, 24 November 11:53:14Z); "delete leftovers." (the same session, 10:40:29Z). The earlier verification runs of this skill, on the user's instruction, treated unused elements and copies of one rule in the program and its tests as damage to remove, and held that "the behaviour is the same" is not by itself a reason to leave them; for repetition in the guides section 3 applies. A file that the program writes into an instance and that nothing reads is a different matter, because removing it changes what a live instance holds: the audit raised three such files, and the user has not answered (section 4.13).

**Repair the instruction, not the agent's freedom.** When agents duplicated work because their briefs told them to, and a session then began building a guard that forbade it, the user said (session `20538f79`, 23 November 01:43:46Z): "you told duplicate workers to do duplicate work, then when they began doing it you tried to add a new ban that would forbid it, when you could simply have told them to just do what they should do in the first place?"

**No compatibility for earlier generations.** The skill does not carry branches for the formats of the third generation and before, migration code or preserved aliases (criterion A4). Section 5.4 describes code that reads what instances of the fourth generation, created earlier this week, still hold. That this code is outside the criterion is this document's reading; it rests on the user having had that code installed together with the removal of the formal judge on 25 November.

**A failure ends the runner; it is not isolated, and nothing restarts the runner.** The program does not pass through an undefined state. A refusal becomes a bounded wait; a failure of the runner's own operation ends the runner with the error recorded, and the operator starts it again after the repair. Isolating the failing piece of work and carrying on, and a watchdog that restarts the runner, are among the directions the user rejected (criteria B5 and B6, and rejected direction R-19 of the criteria file). Sessions that evaluate the skill keep reporting this as a gap; it is a decision.

**The later decision governs** (criterion G4), and a decided matter is not reopened by a correction. When a mechanism the user approved has since acquired exceptions, a repairer who writes a proposal for the user about it should include removing the mechanism among the options; twice this month the user chose a removal that the assistant's options had not offered.

- **The program holds the order of stages and every exit check; no session decides either.** The operator's guide counts seven stages: the start, the selection, the library, the history with the archive, the map with the paper, the documents, and the final check. The library stage has seven steps of its own, the last of which is the freeze. The loops of the hand packaging came from a session deciding the order as it went.
- **The order.** The selection comes first and fixes the main theorem. The history and the archive are made beside the library stage, because they need only the research's record. The paper's draft is made inside the library stage, before the freeze, and its gaps feed a single fold-back into the library. The map comes before the paper. The documents come after the paper, because every figure in them is read by script from finished products. The final check runs once.
- **A product is written once.** A later step compares a product's hash with the run's state before reading it; a difference stops the run and names the product, and nothing is silently made again. "Frozen" means that after the freeze no Lean file and no record changes, and the final check compares their hashes again. When the paper wants a sentence the library does not cover, the sentence is cut or reworded.
- **Only simplifications may be discarded.** A part of the simplifying, arranging or fold-back steps that fails its check is dropped and keeps its previous form. Every other failed exit check stops the run with exit code 2, and the same command continues it. Every count of passes is fixed, with no "repeat until clean", because eight readings of the first paper never converged.
- **The pipeline's and the paper command's sessions cannot schedule themselves.** They are launched without the tools `ScheduleWakeup`, `CronCreate`, `CronDelete`, `CronList` and `Monitor`. The program reads a session's output only after its CLI process has ended, and the CLI does not end while a wake-up is pending: a reading session of a paper run that had set one held the run idle for eleven minutes. The coordinator had this closed at the cause and refused a watchdog that would end a session from outside. Research sessions keep those tools, deliberately: seventeen of 273 sunflower sessions had used them.
- **The first step takes whole elements**, keeps every element of a definition module, everything that carries an attribute or is an `instance`, and everything that is not an element (private declarations, notations, `open` and `variable`), and the definition modules are compared by hash with the instance's first commit after every step that writes Lean. The hand packaging had used a pruning tool that worked on terms and deleted a definition, an `rfl` lemma that `simp` needed, and a local notation.
- **The selection may choose a theorem that the root does not yet rest on.** It then writes the derivation of the root from that theorem as a Lean module, and the program compiles it. The first design had allowed only what the root's existing proof rests on, which would have excluded the user's own example of the better choice. The decision is read once by a second session against the canon.
- **The run takes its settings from the instance's working `settings.json`**, reduced for Lean to what a compile needs, and compiles through a broker of its own, seeded from the instance's cache; there is exactly one Lake build, at the freeze, after a check that every package the build names is present locally at its revision. A Lake build had once fetched Mathlib from the network and compiled 203 of its modules.
- **The form of a history entry is held by the program**: at most 120 words, at most 30 per list item, a heading of number, date and kind, and a line naming the record. The first trial's entries ran to 957 and 236 words and retold the mathematics, against the user's decision on the history.
- **`finalize/` is not under `reference/`** because every file of `reference/` is copied into every research session.
- **PyYAML is required**, and the command refuses at its start without it, because the freeze reads one of the registry's files back.
- **Deliberately not built**: any second check of the frozen library; options for model, effort, output folder or single stages; a README inside the package's `lean/` folder; a check of the classification codes against the registry's list; any submission.

## 5. How the tree came to be what it is

The files this run covers were written, for the most part, in four days. Knowing how they were written tells a repairer where the tree is likely to be uneven, and which of its oddities are deliberate.

### 5.1 The rebuild of 22 November

Until 21 November the skill was in its third generation. In the days before, the user had been watching a third-generation instance work on a conjecture about parking functions (the records call it 주차함수, or "parking"), and had corrected its sessions through a series of standing inputs: a proof that can still be reduced is not yet accepted; judge from the text and not from counts; do not close a statement by large computation done without understanding; and others. ### 6.1 The sunflower instance runs from the files this run edits

The instance at `.skill-workspace/sunflower-20261122` is live. At noon on 25 November it had six sessions running, had published 111 revisions, and its root was unresolved. It is a fourth-generation instance, and it does not run from a copy of the skill: its sessions' commands point at `C:\math\.claude\skills\do-math\runtime\`, and its resume texts are read from `C:\math\.claude\skills\do-math\resume`. The run edits those same files.

How a change reaches the instance depends on what kind of file it is, and `reference/operations.md` states the rules:

- A **guide** (`reference/`, `roles/`, `SKILL.md`) is copied into a session's folder when the session is launched. A session already running keeps the text it was given; every session launched after a change receives the changed text.
- A **resume text** (`resume/`) is read afresh each time a session is resumed. A change reaches sessions that are already running.
- A **command a session calls** (`coordination.py`, `canon_tool.py`, `check.py`, the client side of `lean_broker.py`, and everything they import, such as `canon.py`, `knowledge.py`, `availability.py`, `settings.py` and `runtime_support.py`) starts as a new process each time. A change takes effect at the next call.
- A **session host** is started for each new session from the files as they then stand (`runner.py _session`, with `ending.py`, `retention.py`, `launch.py` and `cli_adapter.py`). A change reaches every session launched after it.
- The **runner** and the **broker** keep the program they loaded when they started. A change to their code does not reach them until they are restarted.

So the instance runs several versions of the program at once, and this is its normal condition. At noon on 25 November the runner held the program as it stood at 00:07 KST that day, when it was last started. By the records that is the program of commit 35: its files were installed with the runner stopped, the runner was started on them, and the commit was made twenty-five minutes later. The broker held the program of 24 November 17:54 KST, older than commit 35: the file in which a broker records its own start lacks a key that every broker started since that commit writes, so the rule that the state file is written at most every ten seconds is not in force in it. Session hosts launched after 10:26 KST run the program with the morning's corrections, and the commands sessions call run the tree as it is. One consequence was worked out by an investigator from the code and has not been observed: the morning's correction that recognises a provider's refusal is in the new session hosts and not in the runner, so a refusal today would be marked as a refusal by the host and still be retried as an ordinary failure by the runner, until the runner is restarted.

An investigator compared the instance's records before and after the two commits of the morning (10:26 and 11:42 KST) and found nothing that broke. No command under `runtime/` printed a traceback of the program in thirty-four session transcripts; every session that ended after 07:42 ended with exit code 0 and no refusal; the instance went on publishing (five publications between 10:32 and 11:32); the broker logged no failure of its own. The commands that failed in those transcripts failed through the sessions' own mistakes, and section 9.3 lists the ones that recur.

The user asked for this run knowing that the instance runs from these files, and the run goes ahead on them; what the lesson quoted at the end of section 5.5 warns against is contained as follows. A change inside the program that alters neither what is written into the instance's files nor what one process passes to another is an ordinary correction. It takes effect for new processes, and the worst it can do is what any mistaken correction does. So is a correction that makes the program do what the guides already promise and leaves alone what the first kind below names: sessions were told to expect that. Three kinds of change, however, touch what the running instance depends on. The freeze document sends the first to the user as a proposal and rules out the other two.

- A change to something that a process already running, or a session already launched, relies on: the form of a file of the instance or of the runner's or the broker's state, or the meaning of what is written there, a settings key or its default, an environment variable given to sessions, and those arguments, output keys and exit codes of a command that another process of the program reads or that the guides promise to a session. What counts is what the guides and the code that reads the value now expect. Where the program gives a value that departs from what both expect, bringing it into line is an ordinary correction. Where a guide promises one thing and the code that reads the value expects another, a change to either side goes to the user. The content of a view that a person or a session reads is not of this kind.
- A resume frame without exactly one of each of its placeholders. `resume/common.md` must contain `{{role_guidance}}`, `{{previous_error}}` and `{{request_id}}` once each, and `resume/output.md` the first two; otherwise the session host stops at its next resumption.
- A link in a guide that leaves the set of files a session is given. When a pack is assembled, a relative link in a guide embedded in it that resolves outside the session's `context/` folder raises an error, and the session cannot be launched. A session's `context/` holds `reference/` with a copy of every file of the skill's `reference/`, `roles/` with the session's own role guide alone, and `SKILL.md`; nothing else.

Code that reads what an instance created by an earlier version of the fourth generation still holds (section 5.4) is in use by this instance, which was created on 22 November. It is not history to be cleared away.

The same rule written in two places that had begun to differ was found in every run, in guides (a table of folders duplicated between two references) and in tests (the same refusal asserted in two modules, copies of a helper). Code that nothing used and branches kept for an earlier format were found in every run. In the broker and the session hosts, defects of ordering between processes were uncovered one layer per round. Use of a term before its definition, one word in two senses, and unclear referents were found in prose in every round. And more than once a stand-in used by the tests hid a real defect that surfaced only in a real run.

**Why proposals were rejected.** In the run of 22 November the judges rejected three proposals, all for the same reason: the defect was real, and the replacement text introduced a new one. One changed how a set was computed and dropped a case the original had handled; one moved information out of a place where a later session still needed it; one, rewording a sentence, used "section" in two senses in it. Two were raised again in the next round with a narrower text and accepted; the third was repaired after the run. The lesson the reports draw: when changing a rule that counts or collects, list the cases the present text handles; when rewording, check the new sentence against the shared vocabulary. Other rejections: a proposal that merged tests and lost an assertion; a proposal resting on a false premise about what a guide lacked; proposals that narrowed something the user had decided, which went to the user instead; and a new mechanism to protect against an operator's mistake, which was closed as needless complexity.

**What repairers got wrong about their own task.** Repairers escalated to the user matters that the files in scope and the sources of the run already decided; that a sentence stands in a document does not make it a user decision, and that several repairs are possible is not a reason to ask. Repairers left a defect unreported because they took it to belong to another repairer's area; areas divide the duty to search, not the right to report. And repairers who found one defect did not look for the same pattern elsewhere; an earlier freeze asked that whoever finds a defect search its own area and the whole tree for the same wrong assumption, order of checks or turn of phrase, and report each place.

**What judging without running cannot see.** Judges do not execute anything. In one run three judges unanimously accepted a repair whose premise about a helper was false, and only a run afterwards showed it. Proposals that are each valid can conflict once applied together. This is why the driving session runs the tests at intervals and reads the result, and why a proposal that changes behaviour carries, in the same proposal, the change to the test that asserts that behaviour.

**The run of the morning of 25 November.** It answered the same request as the present run, on 76 files (the paper command was left out and the finalize pipeline did not yet exist), and it was closed after one round at the user's word, "wrap it up with round 1"; the user gave no reason. Seven repairers raised 74 proposals. Twenty-two of them overlapped and were combined, and three judges judged the resulting 62. Fifty-six were corrections that all three accepted, and they were applied, in 39 files (commit `150b61ab`); two were proposals for the user that all three accepted (section 4.13); four were rejected (section 9.9). Six corrected the program: a turn that the AI provider ended with a refusal was not recognised as a refusal; a candidate build that had failed to compile was kept, so that running the same command again did not build again; the broker overwrote a result, or ended, when Windows briefly refused the removal of a queue file; and three smaller ones. Fifteen corrected guides, among them the reviewer's rule for a minor flaw that the user had asked for on 22 November and that had never reached the guides. Seventeen corrected tests: temporary folders left behind after every run, paths that no test went down, assertions that did not fix the reason for a refusal, costs. Eighteen removed leftovers or corrected comments that described an earlier state.

Three things about that round bear on the present run. First, one round is not a measure of what remains: every earlier run of this skill kept finding defects in its second and later rounds, and the closing report of the morning lists observations that repairers made and did not raise (section 9.9). Second, the closing test run did not finish. It was stopped by a guard against low memory after ten of the twenty-two modules, so several corrections to the program and to tests are in the tree without having been executed (section 8). Third, one defect was found independently by four repairers, each of whom wrote a proposal for the user about it. Proposals for the user carry no replacement text, so the procedure did not combine them, and the judges received four. Two were rejected because the option they recommended rested on a final state that the option would not in fact produce, and the two that were accepted recommended the same direction by different means. A repairer who writes a proposal for the user therefore has to follow each option to what it actually leaves in the files, and the matter those four raised is now waiting for the user and is not to be raised again (section 4.13).

## 11. The repository's criteria for judging a test

The repository keeps a distillation of what its earlier work learned about tests (memory notes `L-0001` and `L-0023`). It was written by AI sessions from past experience and is not a set of user decisions, but the earlier freezes used it and it is the measure this run applies together with the user's requirements of section 3. The points that bear most often on this suite:

- **A passing test testifies only to what it actually exercised.** A stand-in, a fixture or a patched name lets through every defect outside what it models. The broker passed all its tests with stand-ins and then failed on the real compiler at three boundaries the stand-ins did not model: state left by an earlier version, the file semantics of the platform, and how an executable is looked up.
- **A stand-in belongs only at the boundary with something outside the program or with time.** The program's own receiving, storing and judging run as real code. A stand-in does not answer the unknown with success, does not infer what the real producer would compute, and does not fill in what is missing.
- **A test of a refusal fixes the reason for the refusal**, not only that something was refused. Its input starts from what the real producer makes and changes the one thing the test is about, so that the failure can be attributed.
- **Passing does not show that a check is strong.** A check that goes over all members is tried by removing a member and seeing that it fails and names the member. Expected values are computed independently of the code under test.
- **A test tied to a past state is a defect.** A branch that tolerates a known old failure counts the failure's return as a pass; an assertion on a line number fails when correct code moves.
- **Right after tests are reworked, compare the number collected**, and confirm that the test guarding a particular repair is still collected. A test once ended up after the block that runs the module and never ran.
- **Cost.** A time budget means something only where overruns are reported. When cutting a test's cost, keep its starting state and the real path it goes down, not only its assertions; a method without an assertion of its own may still check through a helper or through the absence of an exception. A test that prepares and restores real stored state is not cheap whatever it is called.
- **A test that inherits the program's defaults inherits what is right for operation.** On a loaded machine, tests that inherited the default below-normal priority starved; the fixture was given normal priority and the default was kept.
- **A test that failed once under load** is examined for a race in the program before it is called unreliable; one such failure was a real race, reproduced by constructing the state.
- **A test made from an incident asserts the property the contract must keep**, not the incident's particulars; the user asked for exactly this when tests were written for an incident fix.
- **Counts.** Results of runs of different versions are not added together into "all pass". Skipped and not-run are kept apart from passed.
