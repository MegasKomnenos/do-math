# Fact check 5: README lines 239–355, the running guide and CITATION.cff, after the revision

This report compares three revised texts with the sources of the current do-math implementation: `C:\math\README.md` lines 239 to 355, the whole of `C:\math\docs\do-math\running.md`, and the `message`, `abstract` and `keywords` fields of `C:\math\CITATION.cff`. It is written for the agent that decides what still has to be corrected before the texts are published. An earlier report, `사실대조3.md`, checked the drafts that preceded the revision. This one repeats the comparison on the revised text, says for each earlier finding whether the revision repaired it, and compares the two documents with each other.

The whole range was checked, and each verdict rests on the source file read at the place cited. No statement was found that a source contradicts. Nine statements say more than their source does, one has no source in the repository, and one concerns GitHub and cannot be checked from the repository. Of the fifteen statements that the earlier report listed, twelve are repaired, two are repaired in part and one, about GitHub, could not be checked then or now. The three disagreements that the earlier report found between or inside the two documents are repaired as well.

Four results matter more than the rest, and section 2 gives them first within each document.

- `README.md:294` calls the reading of the root "the only check of what the Lean statement means". The sources call it the only judgment of meaning, and the running guide calls the comparison with known values "the check available to you". The two documents therefore use the word "check" for two different things at the place where the reader decides how far to trust the Lean statement.
- `running.md:294` tells the reader to run the judge of the root by hand "only" in one case. The operating guide names that case and does not exclude others.
- `running.md:298` states, as a property of Windows, that a session's processes are contained. The operating guide makes it a condition that a file in the session folder records, and it names processes that containment never covers.
- The exit code 2 that `running.md:289` and `running.md:294` assign to "findings stayed open" and to "rejected" is also the code with which `paper.py` and `judge.py` end when the command line cannot be parsed. No sentence is wrong for a well-formed command, so this is reported as a remark in section 2.4 and not as a verdict.

Locations are written as `path:line`. `SKILL` abbreviates `C:\math\.claude\skills\do-math`, `OPS` abbreviates `SKILL\reference\operations.md`, `RUNTIME` abbreviates `SKILL\runtime`, `V` abbreviates `C:\math\docs\do-math\lean-canon-verification.md`, and `RUN` abbreviates `C:\math\docs\do-math\running.md`. ## 2. Statements whose verdict is not `supported`

### 2.1 README.md

**R1. `README.md:294` — what the reading of the root is.** Verdict: `overstated`.

> "When it has succeeded, read what was fixed, as the running guide describes: this reading is the only check of what the Lean statement means."

The sources give the reading a narrower description and give the name "check" to something else.

- `OPS:159`: "The user's confirmation is part of fixing the root, and it is the only judgment of meaning the Lean statement will receive from the person whose proposition it is."
- `OPS:142`: "The check against known values is the means you have."
- `RUN:134`: "The check available to you is a comparison with known values."
- `RUN:217`: "This reading is the only judgment of meaning that the Lean statement of the root receives."

The value checks test whether the definitions denote the intended objects, and the definitions decide what the Lean statement means. They are a check of meaning in the documents' own words, limited to the listed values. The reading is the only judgment of the statement as a whole. The README itself keeps this distinction outside the range, at `README.md:225`.

Corrected sentence: "When it has succeeded, read what was fixed, as the running guide describes: this reading is the only judgment that the meaning of the Lean statement receives."

**R2. `README.md:311` — how many sessions proved the root.** Verdict: `overstated` (minor).

> "Its statement was that the sum of the first n odd numbers is n squared, and six sessions carried it to a formally proved root."

Three of the six sessions brought the root to its formal proof. The other three published further elements afterwards.

- `V:65`: "The runner launched six sessions, all on the same model (`claude/opus`, effort high): an attempt, an exploration, a peer for each, and a review for each candidate."
- `V:68`: "The root was formally proved 863 seconds after the attempt started, and the instance counted as complete from then on."
- `V:69`: "The exploration was assigned the root question. Its peer published, 2,377 seconds after the attempt started, nineteen further elements with complete Lean proofs and the first understanding text of the root question."

Corrected sentence: "Its statement was that the sum of the first n odd numbers is n squared. The runner launched six sessions, and the root was formally proved by the first three of them, an attempt, its peer and a review."

**R3. `README.md:353` — what GitHub does with the file.** Verdict: `not checked`.

- G1 above concerns the last sentence but one of the judge's subsection.

The exact wording would be: "its ID is the `pid` under `process` in the instance's `runner/process.json`", and likewise for `runner/lean/process.json`.
