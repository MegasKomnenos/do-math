Where a guide and the code differ, the verdict follows the code and section 3 records the difference.

A statement, for the counts below, is one sentence of prose, one table cell or one diagram label that asserts a fact about the system. A sentence that contains two clauses with different verdicts is counted once, under the more severe verdict, and both clauses are described. Line 8 and the two sentences under "Solved problems" (L29) are outside the check and are not counted.

### Group A

#### A1. "Everything the research keeps" is an element (L5, L15): `overstated`

README, L5 (table, "What it is"): "Everything the research keeps is a declaration of one library written in Lean 4, a proof assistant, with a written account beside it."

README, L15: "Everything the research keeps is an element of that library: a Lean declaration, which is either a definition or a statement, together with a written record that holds what Lean cannot hold, …"

The sources say this of the mathematical assertions, not of everything that is kept.

- `SKILL.md:10`: "Every mathematical assertion the research keeps is an **element**, which is a declaration of that library whose statement Lean has compiled, together with its **record**."
- `reference/common.md:7`: "Every definition and every statement the research keeps is a declaration that Lean has compiled."
- `reference/common.md:101`: the canon "consists of the instance's Lean library, a written record for each declaration of that library, and a small amount of written state attached to particular statements and questions".
- `reference/common.md:131`: "A session learns two kinds of thing. Some of it is mathematics about the objects, and that enters as elements and their records. The rest is about the course of the work … This second kind is kept in two ways", namely the approaches tried on a statement and the understanding so far of a question (`reference/common.md:135`).
- `reference/canon.md:12-22` lists, beside `lean/` and `records/`, the folders `approaches/`, `questions/`, `evidence/`, `history/` and the file `bibliography.bib`.
- `reference/common.md:137`: "The session's own account is kept outside the canon: its report, its conversation and its working files are retained unchanged."

The README itself is exact at L39: "everything the research keeps as a result is an element", after naming the "small amount of written text attached to particular statements and questions". L5 and L15 drop the limit "as a result".

Corrected L5: "Every result the research keeps is a declaration of one library written in Lean 4, a proof assistant, with a written record beside it."

Corrected L15: "Every result the research keeps is an element of that library: a Lean declaration, which is either a definition or a statement, together with a written record that holds what Lean cannot hold, namely a short description, the statement in ordinary mathematical language, an explanation and, where there is one, a proof in words."

(L5 also says "a written account" where every later place says "record"; the corrected sentence uses the README's own term.)

#### A2. "Each addition" passes through three sessions (L5, L17): `overstated`

README, L5: "Each addition is produced by one session, turned into a change to the library by a second and judged by a third, and it is published with that judgment."

README, L17: "Each addition to the library is made in three stages, each by a different session."

Two kinds of addition enter the library without a peer and without a review.

- `reference/common.md:184`, on the organizer: "where two definitions denote one object, or two statements have the same content, it establishes as an element that they agree, with a complete Lean proof. Lean checks that proof, so the element enters without review, with its proof accepted."
- `roles/organizer.md:23`: "No review reads this record … The element enters as accepted." `roles/organizer.md:29` adds that the organizer can also add a one-line derivation of one statement from another that agrees with it, "as a declaration of its own … it is then complete in Lean from the allowed axioms and enters as accepted like the agreement itself."
- `SKILL.md:40`: "Two kinds of proof stand without a review. One is a complete Lean proof you supply with the canon at creation … The other is a statement an organizer adds".
- Code: `runtime/knowledge.py:173-176` gives every declaration an organizer adds the verdict `'correct': 'confirmed', 'accepted': True` with the reason "Proved in Lean from the allowed axioms; added by the organizer."; `runtime/runner.py:543-549` puts an organizer's output into the publication queue directly, with no peer or review job.

The README states this exception itself, outside this range, at L169 ("Nothing an organizer submits goes through a review") and L208. L5 and L17 therefore also disagree with the README's own later text.

Corrected L5: "Each addition that comes from a session's research is produced by one session, turned into a change to the library by a second and judged by a third, and it is published with that judgment."

Corrected L17: "Each addition to the library that comes from a session's research is made in three stages, each by a different session." If the exception is to be visible here, one sentence can follow the paragraph: "Two kinds of addition do not take this path: the definitions and proofs supplied with the root when the instance is created, and the statements that an organizer proves in Lean; the section 'What counts as established' says what stands behind them."

#### A3. The condition for a complete run (L6, L23): `overstated`

README, L6 (table, "When a run is complete"): "Only when the statement, which is fixed in Lean before the research starts, or its negation has a proof that is complete in Lean, uses only the allowed axioms and has been accepted by a review."

README, L23: "do-math considers a run complete only when the Lean statement fixed at the start, or its negation, has a formal proof that a review has accepted."

Both sentences are worded as a necessary condition ("only when"), so neither is false of an ordinary run. They are placed, however, where a reader looks for the definition, and as a definition they differ from the sources in two ways.

First, they drop the condition that everything the proof rests on is settled. The sources define completion through "settled", which is recursive.

- `reference/common.md:170`: "The instance is complete when the root is settled through a proof that is formal, or when a statement that Lean has confirmed to be the negation of the root is."
- `reference/common.md:166`: "A proof is **settled** when it is accepted and every statement of the canon that it names is settled, and a statement is settled when one of its proofs is." The same paragraph says of a proof that is correct and held back: "The element is published, and other proofs can use it. What it cannot do is make them settled."
- Code: `runtime/knowledge.py:295-298` adds a proof to the settled set only when its standing is `accepted` and every statement it names has a settled proof; `runtime/knowledge.py:326-327` makes a statement formally settled only through a proof that is both settled and formal; `runtime/knowledge.py:348-351` and `:423-424` derive completion from that.

A root proof that is complete in Lean, uses only the allowed axioms and has been accepted by a review therefore does not complete the run while a lemma it names is "correct, held back", even if that lemma's Lean proof is complete too. The README gives the exact definition at L76.

Second, "accepted by a review" has an exception. An organizer's one-line derivation of a statement from another statement that agrees with it enters as accepted without a review (`roles/organizer.md:29`; `runtime/knowledge.py:173-176`), and `runtime/canon.py:1113-1116` requires of an organizer's added theorem only that it be proved in Lean from the allowed axioms. I found no rule that excludes the root from this. If the root were derived in this way from a settled, formally proved statement that agrees with it, the root would be settled through a proof that no review accepted, although a review accepted the proof of the statement it was derived from. This is an edge case and the lesser half of the finding.

Corrected L6: "Only when the statement, which is fixed in Lean before the research starts, or its negation has a proof that is complete in Lean, uses only the allowed axioms and is accepted, and every statement that proof rests on is settled in the same way."

Corrected L23: "do-math considers a run complete only when the Lean statement fixed at the start, or its negation, is settled through a formal proof: the proof is accepted, and every statement it names has an accepted proof in turn, all the way down."

In both corrected sentences "accepted" replaces "accepted by a review"; the sentence of L23 that follows ("Every review is an AI session…") and the table at L67-72 already say who accepts.

#### A4. "The views show units" (L55): `overstated`

README, L55: "The views show units and pass through a step whose proof is accepted, so that a reader sees the structure of the proof and not every lemma; a step remains an element, and proofs name it."

The sentence also conflicts with L80 of the README, which says that the index "lists every element of the canon".

Corrected L55: "The frontier shows units and passes through a step whose proof is accepted, showing in its place what that step rests on, so that a reader sees the structure of the proof and not every lemma; a step remains an element, proofs name it, and the index lists it."

#### B1. Compiling and extracting "at every publication" (L19, L57, L103): `overstated`

README, L57: "At every publication the runner extracts from the compiled library a **structural index**: for each declaration, the text of its statement, what the statement and the proof use, whether the Lean proof is complete, and the axioms the proof depends on."

L57 repeats the wording of `reference/common.md:113` ("At each publication the runner … extracts a **structural index** from the compiled library by machine"), and L19 that of `reference/common.md:125`; the operating guide and the code are narrower (section 3, G1). A candidate that only adds an approach entry or replaces an understanding text is the ordinary case in which nothing is compiled.

Corrected L57: "Whenever a publication changes the Lean files, the runner extracts again from the compiled library the **structural index** of the changed modules and of every module that imports them: for each declaration, the text of its statement, what the statement and the proof use, whether the Lean proof is complete, and the axioms the proof depends on."

Corrected L19: "It applies the change to the library, compiles the library when the change touches a Lean file, and commits the result, and the review's judgment of each element stays attached to that element with its reason."

Corrected L103: "publication: the change is applied, the library compiled if a Lean file changed, the result committed"

Corrected L78: "A line of the frontier gives the statement's name, the short name from its record or, where the record has none, its description, its standing, the beginning of the review's reason where the proof is not confirmed or is held back, the number of approaches that have been tried on it, and the number of sessions that hold a section on it."

## 3. Disagreements between a guide and the code that bear on a README sentence

**G1. Extraction and compilation "at each publication".** `reference/common.md:113` says "At each publication the runner … extracts a **structural index** from the compiled library", and `reference/common.md:125` that the runner "applies the candidate to the canon, compiles the library, extracts the structural index again and commits the result." The code compiles and extracts only when Lean files change (`runtime/runner.py:782-787`, `:805`, `:751-754`, `:918`), and `reference/operations.md:467` agrees with the code. Bears on L19, L57 and L103 (finding B1). The README follows `common.md`.

**G2. What "held by N sessions" counts.** `reference/common.md:201` says "the number of sessions that hold sections on it". The code counts pipelines: `runtime/knowledge.py:642-645` adds one for each board row that lists the element, and `runtime/coordination.py:105-106` gathers into one row the sections of the producer, of the peer and of earlier peers of the same pipeline. A producer and its peer that both registered sections on one statement count as one. Bears on L78 (finding B2); the corrected sentence there follows the guide's wording, and "pipelines that hold a section on it" would follow the code exactly.

**G3. Who writes the end authorization at the limit.** `reference/retained-work.md:74` says "The runner records end authorization after a valid decision or after the configured maximum number of resumptions. At the maximum it authorizes the handoff itself, recording `source: runner`", and `reference/common.md:307` says "at the limit the runner records its own end authorization". In the code the session host writes that record: `runtime/ending.py:239-246` runs inside the host's `supervise` loop (`runtime/runtime_support.py:445-447`) and calls `authorize(session, state, 'runner', 'resumption_limit', …)`. The runner process writes an authorization itself only when the host was lost (`runtime/runner.py:414-424`). Bears on L129: "or when the limit is reached and the runner authorizes the end". The README follows the guides and the wording of the record (`source: runner`), and I counted the sentence as supported; because L129 presents the session host as a process separate from the runner, "and the end is authorized without a decision from the session" would be exact.

**G4. The lease's endings.** `reference/sections.md:36` names three ("until you register, release or end your session"); `reference/sections.md:34` and `runtime/coordination.py:235-240` give a fourth. This is a difference between two sentences of one guide, settled by the code. Bears on L123 (finding B3).

**G5. The index and definitions.** `reference/canon.md:26` and `SKILL.md:56` say that `index.md` gives every element "with its current standing" (in `SKILL.md`: "each element with its self-description and standing"). A definition has no standing (`reference/common.md:160`), and the code prints a definition's kind in that place (`runtime/knowledge.py:663-664`). Bears on L80: "lists every element of the canon by module with its standing". Counted as supported, since the guides say it in these words; "with the standing of each statement" would be exact.

**G6. "The root is the first statement of the library."** `SKILL.md:10` says so, and L39 repeats it. The code does not require it: the canon supplied at creation may contain other theorems, such as checks of the definitions with complete Lean proofs (`runtime/runner.py:153-155`, "The theorems the canon already proves in Lean are judged like those a publication brings"; `SKILL.md:40`), and these stand in the definitions' module, which the root's module imports (`reference/canon.md:59`). Counted as supported on the guide's wording; the sentence is true if "first" is read as "the statement the library starts from", and not if it is read as "the only statement at the start".

No other difference between a guide and the code was met in the places read for this range. In particular the following README sentences were confirmed in the code as well as in the guides: the runner's refusal of a changed root (`runtime/runner.py:205-208`), the requirement that the root enter without a proof (`runtime/runner.py:180-183`), the four standings and the definition of "settled" (`runtime/knowledge.py:226-238`, `:286-300`), the frontier's construction (`runtime/knowledge.py:472-525`), the refusals of the lease and section commands (`runtime/coordination.py:202-217`, `:358-374`), the default of three resumptions and the roles to which reconsideration applies (`runtime/settings.py:38`; `runtime/ending.py:18`, `:23`, `:239`), and the handling of interrupted sessions (`runtime/ending.py:196-199`; `runtime/runner.py:458-488`, `:491-503`, `:396-402`).

## 6. Terms

**Definitions.** Each term that the range sets in bold was compared with the sources' definition: instance, root, canon, record, element, statement, imported library, weight, unit, step, structural index, standings, work that remains, settled, formal, frontier, index, attempt, explorer, formalizer, peer, review, organizer, pipeline, retained work, section, board, lease, role guide and session host. All are defined as the sources define them, with one reservation: the gloss of **formalizer** at L84 gives the usual case as the definition (finding B4).

**Terms used in two senses within the range.** Five were found. None makes a sentence false; the first two can mislead.

1. **statement.** L39 defines a statement as an element ("A **statement** is an element too: in Lean it is a `theorem`"). The range also uses the word for what a declaration asserts, including what a definition's declaration contains: "for each declaration, the text of its statement, what the statement and the proof use" (L57), "The statement of a published element never changes" (L19), "whether its Lean statement says what was meant" (L17), and the record part "Statement" (L50). The two senses separate at L74 and L78, where a statement has several proofs ("one of its proofs", "lists the root's proofs"): an element is one declaration with one proof, and the sources explain that "A statement can also have further proofs, each a further declaration with the same statement" (`reference/common.md:162`). The README, in this range and as far as a search of the rest shows, does not say that a further proof is a further declaration, so a reader who holds to the definition at L39 cannot tell how one element has several proofs. One sentence after L74, in the sources' words, would close the gap.
2. **step.** L55 defines a **step** as an element of weight "step". L15 ("as a step that some argument leaves open") and L39 ("an open step of the root's proof") use the word, before the definition, for any open statement of a proof, which may well be a unit. The source of L39 says "an open piece of the root's proof" (`reference/common.md:105`). L115 ("the steps that still have to be proved") uses the ordinary sense after the definition. Writing "piece" at L15 and L39, as the source does, removes the collision.
3. **section.** L119 defines a **section** as what an attempt takes on. L35 ("the rest of this section describes"), L39 and L65 ("the section 'What counts as established'") use the word for a part of the README. The context separates the senses.
4. **views.** L35 uses "the views" for the files the runner derives in `knowledge/`; L49 ("Views that list many elements") uses it for the output of the reading commands, as the guides do. Finding A4 is the place where the difference matters.
5. **index.** Two bold terms share the word: the **structural index** (L57, `canon/index.json`) and the **index** (L80, `knowledge/index.md`). The range keeps them apart by always writing "structural index" for the first. L51 also uses "index" in the mathematical sense ("where an index starts from zero"), and L80 gives a "standing" to every element although only statements have one (section 3, G5).

One observation crosses the boundary of this range. L125 defines the **role guide** as "the guide that each role receives". L159, outside this range, writes "The guide that every role receives" for a different document, the common introduction (`reference/common.md`), which is where the description of progress it cites is found. The two phrases are nearly identical and name two guides.
