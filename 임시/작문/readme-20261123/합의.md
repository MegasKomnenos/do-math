## What the reader should understand after reading the README

Compressed statement. In do-math many AI sessions that never converse research one statement, and everything they keep is a declaration of one Lean library with a written record beside it; each piece of work is produced by one session, turned into a change to the library by a second, and judged by a third, and it is published with that judgment. What a published result can be trusted for depends on the standing of its proof and on whether the proof is complete in Lean. A run is complete only when the Lean statement fixed at the start, or its negation, has a formal proof, and whether that Lean statement says what the user meant is settled by the user before the run starts and checked by nothing afterwards.

Heavy:

- The knowledge of a run as a Lean library of elements with records, and how the three stages (producing session, peer, review) add to it without any two sessions conversing.
- What each state of a result certifies and what it does not: the four standings, settled, formal, the judge, and the limit that nothing after the start checks the correspondence between the Lean statement and the user's meaning.

Light (needed to follow, verify or act on the heavy items):

- The six roles in detail, sections and leases, how a session ends, questions and what counts as progress, the two documents on research and writing, the formalizer, the organizer, the runner and the Lean broker.
- Concerns, findings and comprehensive review.
- How to read the results of a run on disk.
- Requirements, what a run does on the machine, and the quick start.
- Repository layout, what was verified and the tests.
- The standalone tools and supporting skills, language, citation and licence.
- The solved-problems section (empty).

Left out: the previous implementation and how the current one differs from it, the parking-function runs, Zenodo, the roadmap, the history of the design, running instances and their state.

## What the reader should be able to do after reading the running guide

Compressed statement. To run do-math you fix the statement twice, in words and in Lean, together with the operator; you prepare a folder with the Lean definitions, the root statement and a record for each declaration, and a settings file; `init` compiles that library and refuses what does not meet its conditions; you read and confirm what was fixed, and only then `start`. The sessions then run unattended under your account with full file and command access and with no cap on time, sessions or usage, until the root is formally settled or you end the run.

Heavy: the path from a statement to a running instance, including the user's confirmation; what the sessions can do on the machine and what has no cap.

Light: requirements, settings and their defaults, the commands, standing inputs, the tools that produce the dependency map, the paper and the judge's verdict, ending a run, interruptions and completion.

Left out: everything the operating guide in the skill covers in full and a user does not need before a first run; the previous commands.

The readers receive the documents themselves and nothing else. Links to the guides in the skill are part of the documents, and the readers are not given the guides.
