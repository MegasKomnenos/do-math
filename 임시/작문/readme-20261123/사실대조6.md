Nothing outside those 70 passages was checked again, except where a replacement had to be compared with another sentence of the documents for part 3.

All 70 replacements stand in the documents exactly once, and each was read there with the paragraph around it. No statement is contradicted by a source. > "The views described below use the weight to show the structure of a proof and not every lemma; a step remains an element, and proofs name it."

The text that this replaced said it of the frontier and added that the list of all elements includes a step. The replacement says it of "the views described below". The dependency map of the research draws units and folds each step into the connections that pass through it, and the paper … numbers units and absorbs steps into the proofs that use them."
- `RUNTIME\knowledge.py:517-518`: the frontier passes through a statement only when `target['weight'] == 'step' and target['standing'] == 'accepted'` and it has one proof.

**R4. `README.md:164` — when the one-line derivation is put in place.** Verdict: `overstated` (minor).

> "where the old statement follows from the new one, the old element's proof becomes a one-line derivation from it, at once if the old statement was not settled and otherwise once the new statement is settled as well."

The first half of the added clause is supported: for an old statement that is not settled, the candidate that adds the new statement also replaces the old proof (`SKILL\reference\change-format.md:102`). The second half reads as if the replacement happened when the new statement becomes settled. The sources say that it becomes permitted then, and that it happens only if a later candidate makes it.

- `SKILL\reference\change-format.md:104`: "So your candidate adds the new statement and leaves the proof of the old one as it is. Once the new statement is settled, the derivation can replace the old proof, in a later candidate of any peer. … Review then confirms and accepts the derivation, or leaves the old proof in place."
- `V:145`: "No session is launched to take this second step. A peer whose work comes to the old statement can take it, and until one does the old proof remains."
- `RUNTIME\check.py:139-143`: the check refuses the earlier order and triggers nothing: "Publish the new statement first; the derivation of the old statement from it replaces the old proof once the new statement is settled".
- `SKILL\reference\change-format.md:106` adds a case in which the derivation is not put in the old proof's place at all: "When the new declaration's module imports the module of the old element, the one-line derivation cannot be put in place … It then enters as a new declaration with `proves` naming the old statement."

One role guide has the README's shorter form (`SKILL\roles\peer.md:59`: "An old statement that is already settled keeps its proof until the new statement is settled as well …; the derivation replaces the old proof after that"). The format reference, the verification record and the code are the more exact sources, and they agree with one another.

Corrected sentence: "where the old statement follows from the new one, the old element's proof becomes a one-line derivation from it: at once if the old statement was not settled, and otherwise only after the new statement is settled as well, when a later candidate makes the replacement."
