## What the reader should come away with

From Excerpt 1 (U2):

1. Being stuck on an important obstacle and thinking about it counts as good work, also without a promise of return, and the user prefers it to a sure result that is worth nothing.
2. The session is not pressed for time. It should not waste time, and it can take the time hard work needs.
3. The user wants a good result. A half-baked result, a result that came from avoiding needed work, and one that simply gave up and says so are not that.
4. The reader should not conclude that an honest account of a limit reached after real effort is unwanted, or that partial work honestly stated is unwanted. (The guides ask for both elsewhere; the excerpt must not read against them.)

From Excerpt 2, the session that continues (U1, U3, R1 to R3):

From Excerpt 3 (U1, U2):

11. On handing over, the session leaves to the new session how far it pursues a direction and when it ends, because that session has a whole conversation and is not pressed for time.

## Compressed statement

A session can stay stuck on what is hard and think, without pressure of time, and that is worth more than a sure result worth nothing. A session that hands its task over writes for a successor that has a full conversation and its own judgment of effort and ending, and a session that continues carries the earlier output over first and then keeps one report of the whole task as it stands.
