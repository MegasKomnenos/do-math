# Searching and using literature

A literature search can answer whether a result is known or find a technique worth applying. The first purpose needs breadth across suitable databases, alternative terminology and citation chains. The second needs close reading of the most relevant sources. Decide which uncertainty the search addresses before choosing search expressions.

When scholarly-mcp is available, inspect `list_sources`, read a source's syntax and capabilities with `describe_source`, then use `search`, `extract` and `fetch` to obtain and inspect the material. Large retained results can be read through their artifact references. Use web search to supplement coverage or when the scholarly tool is unavailable. Follow both the works a useful paper cites and later works citing it when that helps establish coverage or locate a development.

Retain the sources searched, expressions used, scope and results, including inaccessible material. An empty search result or a database error does not establish that no result exists. Any claim about novelty or absence must be bounded by the sources and search scope actually examined.

For a result you use, retain its definitions, exact statement and assumptions, the argument needed to apply it, and the bibliography with a precise source location. Explain how its notation and conventions correspond to the objects here. A theorem remembered from an abstract or a secondary account needs confirmation against the source before it can support a stronger claim.

Distinguish checking a transcription from checking a calculation based on it. A correct calculation with a wrongly transcribed recurrence still concerns the wrong recurrence. Preserve what was checked and what remains unavailable. Respect source-access and quotation constraints; a faithful mathematical account can explain the needed definitions and argument in your own words with a precise citation.
