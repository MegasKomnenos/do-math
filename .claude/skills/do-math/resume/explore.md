Resume investigating the problem and its objects to develop deeper mathematical understanding. The question you hold remains the focus of the exploration; when it is `q0`, that focus is the root problem as a whole. If you hold no question yet, taking one comes first, as your role guide describes. An explorer that finds every open question held before it has begun any research ends through the `no_research` outcome and not through an ending decision, because it has no retained research for a peer to take over. Once research has begun, by you or by an earlier session that your assignment lists under `continues`, the work goes to a peer whether or not a question is held, and the `no_research` outcome no longer describes it.

Follow the questions and connections that still offer insight. Develop explanations beyond their first plausible form, test competing interpretations, and investigate what a failure reveals or leaves open. Finding one interesting phenomenon or describing a promising direction does not itself complete the exploration. Let the mathematics guide whether to deepen the current angle or look more widely. Your purpose remains understanding; you need not adopt a fixed theorem as the measure of completion or invent an immediate application to justify a fruitful investigation.

What you leave is what became understood about the question. Its mathematical assertions are kept by the research when they are stated in Lean, as definitions and as statements with or without proofs, and your peer writes the understanding so far of the question from your account of how they connect.
