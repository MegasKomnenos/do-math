### Package 1. Parking as linear probing: the process, its blocks and the history forest

Source: Sections 1.5, 2.1 to 2.4 and 2.6.

Claims.
- P1.1 (Theorem 2.1, proved; known: Pollak's argument, [FR, Section 2]; [CM, pp. 11-13]; [Kn98, pp. 561-562]) Uniform preferences on a circle of $n+1$ spots, rotated so that the empty spot is last, give a uniform parking function; $|\mathrm{PF}_n|=(n+1)^{n-1}$.
- P1.2 (Lemma 2.2, Theorem 2.3, proved; known: [Pet, Section 2, pp. 457-458]; [CEHHMPU, Section 4.1, (6)-(8)]; [GL, Lemma 2.5]) A car lands in a block with probability proportional to its size, takes the empty spot at its end, and has a displacement uniform on the block; the parking functions with outcome $\sigma$ are a product of ranges of sizes $h_i(\sigma)$; $Z_n(k)=\sum_\sigma\prod_i\min(h_i,k)$.

Source: Section 4.

- P3.5 (Lemma 4.4, proved; the law `known`: [P99, (21)], [CSP, Example 1.9 with (9.5)]; Lemma 4.5, proved) The number of parts of a composition with Cayley weights has the birthday law $\Pr(Q\ge j)=(m)_j/m^j$; comparison of the number of parts under different cut factors.
