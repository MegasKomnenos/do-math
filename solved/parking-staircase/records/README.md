# The records of the proof of unimodality through the staircase

This folder holds written records for the Lean proof in [`../lean/`](../lean/), which proves the unimodality of parking functions by maximum displacement through a stronger statement, the staircase. A record is a text for one public declaration of the proof: its statement in ordinary mathematical language, an explanation, and, for a theorem, its proof in words.

The proof rests on 445 public declarations: the 106 of the modules under `../lean/Staircase/`, and 339 of the modules of the library `Parking` under `../lean/Parking/`. (With the private declarations behind them it rests on 1,251, as `../lean/README.md` reports; records are written for public declarations only.) This folder holds 443 records, each at the path that follows the declaration's name (`Parking/staircase.md` for `Parking.staircase`). Three of the 445 declarations have no record of their own, the constructor and the two fields of the structure `Parking.IsWSol`, and the unimodality theorem has two records, as the paragraph after the list explains. The records are of three kinds.

- **107 records written for the staircase modules**: one for each of their 106 public declarations, and `Parking/unimodal_via_staircase_proof.md`. The record of the staircase, `Parking/staircase.md`, tells the proof as a whole.
- **142 records of declarations of `Parking`** whose explanations say how this proof uses the statement. They were written for the library and their explanations were then adjusted; the uses were read from the compiled modules. Their statements and proofs are as they were written for the library, with two exceptions, which were made so that no record names a declaration outside the 445: in `Parking/b.md` the statement says in words what a parking function is, and in `Parking/Tq_ratio.md` one sentence of the proof was removed.
- **194 records of declarations of `Parking`** as they were written for the library. Four of them were adjusted in one passage each on 25 November 2026, so that what they say of the uses of a statement holds for this proof: `Parking/FirstLayer/zrT_diag_convex.md`, `Parking/ren_conv.md`, `Parking/poissonMass.md` and `Parking/Tab/allRange_sound.md`.

The unimodality theorem has two records because the records follow a packaging of the sources in which the runtime of a research system compiled the proof, which [`../lean/README.md`](../lean/README.md) describes under "What was checked, and how". In `../lean/` the theorem is `Parking.unimodal_via_staircase`, proved in `../lean/Staircase/Main.lean`. In the packaged library that name belongs to the unimodality statement posed as the proposition to be proved, with its proof left open, and the proved theorem is carried under the name `Parking.unimodal_via_staircase_proof`, with the identical Lean statement. Accordingly `Parking/unimodal_via_staircase.md` is the record of the proposition as it was posed: it has no proof, and it reads the Lean statement, part by part, against the conjecture as it is stated in words. `Parking/unimodal_via_staircase_proof.md` is the record of the proved theorem, with the proof and, in its section "Earlier work", an account of the literature. A reader who comes from `../lean/` therefore finds the proof of its theorem `Parking.unimodal_via_staircase` in the second of these two files.

The bibliography of the records is [`bibliography.bib`](bibliography.bib).

## How the records were made and checked

The 107 records for the staircase modules were written by several AI sessions from the Lean proofs, private lemmas included. Every one of them was compared with the Lean, statement by statement and proof by proof, by a session that had not written it. A record that was changed afterwards, because a proof was simplified or because a module of `Parking` was corrected, was compared again by such a session: whole where its statement or its proof had changed, and otherwise in the sentences that had changed. The comparisons corrected the records where they differed from the Lean and where a remark in an explanation was not true. Figures from floating-point evaluations that explanations quote were recomputed by the comparing sessions or by a separate session, and are marked in the records as not part of the proof. The statements and proofs of the 336 records of declarations of `Parking` are those written for the library, and they were not compared with the Lean again for this folder.

Apart from seventeen of them, the 249 records of the first two kinds were also read whole by sessions that had written or adjusted none of them. These sessions checked every explanation against the account of the whole proof in `Parking/staircase.md` and against the uses read from the compiled modules, and corrected the sentences that did not agree with them. Thirteen of the seventeen are the account of the proof and the records closest to it: `Parking/staircase.md`, the two records of the unimodality theorem, and the records of `column_rule`, `diagonal_rule`, `diagonal_rule_small`, `step_pos_beyond`, `curve_crossing`, `positive_row_early`, `one_sign_change`, `step_persists`, `step_persists_iter` and `step_pos_large`. In place of that reading, a session that had written none of the thirteen compared each of them whole with the Lean, including what rests on what. The other four did not exist in their present form at that reading (`Parking/Kpoly_second_diff.md`, `Parking/curvSum_eq_sum.md`, `Parking/factorial_lt_robbins.md`, `Parking/renewal_logConvex.md`). The first two are records of staircase modules and were compared with the Lean as the previous paragraph says; the other two are records of declarations of `Parking`, whose statements and proofs are those written for the library.

The 194 records of the third kind were read on 25 November 2026 by a session that had written none of them: the explanation of each in full, and its statement and proof for the names they contain. The session looked for sentences that do not hold for this proof, because they tell a use that this proof does not make or give a count of uses that is not the count here. It found the four passages named above, which were then adjusted from the uses that the compiled modules show. Two sentences that are true and do not mention the tables of the staircase modules were left as they are, in `Parking/Tab/glue.md` and `Parking/Tab/nth.md`. A script confirms that the 443 records are present and well formed, and that none of them names a declaration other than the 445 and the proved theorem `Parking.unimodal_via_staircase_proof`.

On 25 November 2026 two of the records written for the staircase modules were corrected, `Parking/curv_ge_pair.md` and `Parking/one_sign_change.md`. Each had, at the end of its statement, a remark that is true by the definitions and that the Lean declaration does not state: in the first, that both sides of the inequality are zero for $R = 0$; in the second, that $b(k,k) = 0$ and what the step in the row $k$ therefore is. The comparisons described above had passed both remarks, because they are true. They were found when the numbered statements of the paper in [`../paper/`](../paper/) were compared with the declarations. The two remarks now stand in the explanations, which say that the statements do not assert them, and the statement of `Parking/curv_ge_pair.md` also says how it reads for $k = 0$, where the subtraction of natural numbers stops at zero. A session that had not made the corrections then compared the two records with the Lean: the whole statement of each, clause by clause, and the sentences that had moved. The cards of the two declarations on the dependency map carry the corrected statements.

On the same day the section "Earlier work" of `Parking/unimodal_via_staircase_proof.md` was corrected in four places, after the works that the paper cites had been opened at the places cited. The theorem of Pappe is about the generating polynomial of the sum of the preferences, which his note calls essentially equivalent to the displacement. Selig and Zhu say in their Remark 2.3 that maximum displacement and height can differ. Ager-Hart and her coauthors count the rankings in which at most $\ell+1$ competitors tie at a rank, and do not call them parking functions with a bounded displacement. And the limit theorem of Petersson is cited from the summary of his doctoral thesis, which was read, where the record had cited the journal article, which could not be opened. A session that had not written the corrections compared the changed sentences with what had been read in the works. What the section says of the other works was not read against the works again on that day.

No person has read the records.
