## Description

The renewal sequence of a positive log-convex kernel is log-convex: if $f_i>0$ and $f_{j+2}^2\le f_{j+1}f_{j+3}$, then $u_{n+1}^2\le u_nu_{n+2}$ for all $n$.

## Statement

Let $f$ and $u$ be real sequences such that $u$ is the renewal sequence of the kernel $f$ (`Parking.IsRenewal`): $u_0=1$ and $u_n=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_iu_{n-i}$ for $n\ge1$. Suppose that $f_i>0$ for every $i\ge1$ and that $f_{j+2}^2\le f_{j+1}f_{j+3}$ for every $j\ge0$, that is, that the sequence $f_1,f_2,f_3,\dots$ is log-convex. Then
$$u_{n+1}^2\le u_n\,u_{n+2}\qquad\text{for every } n\ge0.$$

## Explanation

The statement transfers log-convexity from a kernel to the sequence generated by it. Nothing is assumed about the total mass $\sum f_i$. The Lean docstring attributes the statement to Kaluza (1928) and to de Bruijn and Erdős (1953, Theorem 1); the library proves it in full, by the argument below, and the sources were not consulted for this record.

It is the reason why the extreme-column bound holds (`Parking.extreme_column_le`): the comparison made there, between the feedback of an arbitrary sequence $v$ whose innovations $v_s-\sum_{i=1}^{s}f_iv_{s-i}$ are nonnegative and the feedback of the renewal sequence itself, comes down to cross-ratio inequalities $u_su_{\rho-m}\le u_{s-m}u_\rho$ for the renewal sequence, which are its log-convexity (`Parking.logConvex_cross`). In the persistence argument the kernel is the frozen kernel `Parking.fker`, whose log-convexity is `Parking.fker_logconvex`.

The hypothesis is log-convexity of the kernel from the index $1$ on; $f_0$ plays no role. All inequalities are written in product form, so that no division occurs.

## Proof

By `Parking.renewal_pos` all $u_n$ are positive. The claim $u_{n+1}^2\le u_nu_{n+2}$ is proved by strong induction on $n$.

*The case $n=0$.* The renewal equation gives $u_1=f_1$ and $u_2=f_1u_1+f_2=f_1^2+f_2$, so $u_1^2=f_1^2\le f_1^2+f_2=u_0u_2$.

*The case $n=M+1$.* Assume the claim for all indices $\le M$. Two facts on ratios are used. For a positive sequence $a$ with $a_{j+1}^2\le a_ja_{j+2}$ for all $j<N$, one has $a_{i+1}a_k\le a_ia_{k+1}$ for $i\le k\le N$ (induction on $k$, as in the proof of `Parking.logConvex_cross`). Applied to the kernel, with the index shifted by one, this gives
$$f_{i+2}\,f_{M+2}\le f_{i+1}\,f_{M+3}\qquad(0\le i\le M+1).$$
Applied to $u$, whose log-convexity is known at the indices $\le M$ by the induction hypothesis, it gives
$$u_{j+1}\,u_{M+1}\le u_j\,u_{M+2}\qquad(0\le j\le M+1).$$
Consider the sum over the pairs $(i,j)$ of natural numbers with $i+j=M$,
$$S=\sum_{i+j=M}\bigl(f_{M+3}f_{i+1}-f_{M+2}f_{i+2}\bigr)\bigl(u_{M+2}u_j-u_{M+1}u_{j+1}\bigr).$$
By the two displayed inequalities both factors of every summand are nonnegative, so $S\ge0$.

On the other hand $S$ can be evaluated with the renewal equation. Splitting off the first or the last term of the renewal equations at $M+1$, $M+2$ and $M+3$ gives
$$\sum_{i+j=M}f_{i+1}u_j=u_{M+1},\qquad\sum_{i+j=M}f_{i+1}u_{j+1}=u_{M+2}-f_{M+2},$$
$$\sum_{i+j=M}f_{i+2}u_j=u_{M+2}-f_1u_{M+1},\qquad\sum_{i+j=M}f_{i+2}u_{j+1}=u_{M+3}-f_1u_{M+2}-f_{M+3}.$$
Expanding the product in $S$ and inserting these four sums,
$$S=f_{M+3}u_{M+2}u_{M+1}-f_{M+3}u_{M+1}\bigl(u_{M+2}-f_{M+2}\bigr)-f_{M+2}u_{M+2}\bigl(u_{M+2}-f_1u_{M+1}\bigr)+f_{M+2}u_{M+1}\bigl(u_{M+3}-f_1u_{M+2}-f_{M+3}\bigr),$$
and all terms cancel except
$$S=f_{M+2}\bigl(u_{M+1}u_{M+3}-u_{M+2}^2\bigr).$$
Since $S\ge0$ and $f_{M+2}>0$, $u_{M+2}^2\le u_{M+1}u_{M+3}$, which is the claim for $n=M+1$.
